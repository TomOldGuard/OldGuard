Notes

The oldest imaging trick there is — a room, a pinhole, the world cast upside-down on the far wall — handed to the browser. Here the live camera is sampled down to a coarse grid and each cell is redrawn as a single mark: a monospace glyph chosen by how dark it is, or a halftone dot grown to match. Step back and a face reassembles out of punctuation; lean in and it dissolves into type.

Switch the mark between ASCII glyphs and Halftone dots, and the ink between Mono and Coral. Resolution sets how fine the grid is sampled; Contrast pushes the midtones apart; Invert swaps light for dark and Mirror flips the selfie view. Save frame writes the current still to a PNG. Nothing is uploaded — the feed is read, drawn and discarded frame by frame, entirely on your machine.
